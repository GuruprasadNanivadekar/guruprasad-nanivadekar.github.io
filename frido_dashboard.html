Biggest opportunity

Demand sits at the low end, money sits at the high end. Products priced ₹1,500 or less hold 49% of reviews but only 13% of review-weighted value. Just three products above ₹5,000 (the mattress topper and two chairs) hold 13% of reviews and 63% of the value. Frido has a cheap entry product that people buy and review, and a premium tier that carries the rupees. The gap between them is the opportunity: bundles and upgrade paths that move a ₹999 buyer toward the ₹7,000+ products.

Worth a look: women's footwear

The Men's Dual Strap Sandals rate 4.9 with 628+ reviews. The Women's Arch Comfort Sandals, from the same line, rate 4.6 with 328+ reviews: lower rating, about half the reviews, and a lighter discount (27% vs 38%). It ties with the Cold & Hot Therapy Cap for the lowest rating in the set. Reading the women's sandal reviews for fit and comfort complaints is the quickest fix on the list.

Worth a look: car comfort discounts

Car products average a 68% discount, against 19% on orthotics and 26% on chairs. The Car Comfort Bundle lists at ₹10,698 and sells at ₹2,999. When the list price is one almost nobody pays, "% off" tells shoppers nothing. Testing a lower list price with a smaller discount on this category would show whether the deep discount is actually driving the 676+ reviews.

Reading the ratings and the new launches

Ratings are flat (4.6 to 4.9 everywhere), so review count is the signal that separates products. Two products tagged "New", the Posture Corrector Pro and the Heel Protector, already have 410+ and 510+ reviews at 4.8, which is fast traction. By contrast the Adjustable Wedge Max Cushion has 6 reviews, and the Comfort Formal Shoes with Arch (₹4,999) shows no rating yet, so those two are too new to judge.

Where these numbers come from

Review counts are shown on the storefront as floors ("410+"), so totals are lower bounds. The grid is a curated set of featured products, not the full catalogue, so category shares describe these 20 and not all of Frido. Reviews are a proxy for demand: they are not unit sales or revenue. A live version on Frido's own order data would give real units, margin by product and repeat purchase.
